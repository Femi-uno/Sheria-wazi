Article 49 of the Constitution sets out the rights of an arrested person. They apply to everyone.

- You must be told promptly, in a language you understand, the reason for your arrest.
- You have the right to remain silent, and to be told the consequences of not remaining silent.
- You cannot be forced to make a confession or admission that could be used against you.
- You have the right to communicate with an advocate and with other people who may assist you.
- You must be brought before a court as soon as reasonably possible, and not later than 24 hours. If the 24 hours end outside ordinary court hours or on a day that is not an ordinary court day, the deadline is the end of the next court day.
- At that first appearance, the court must release you on bond or bail, on reasonable conditions, unless there are compelling reasons not to.

Article 50 adds fair trial rights, such as being presumed innocent and being told the charge in detail.
